\begin{abstract}
Cryptoassets are commonly described by architecture or marketing label, neither of which fully states how a token is used or how value can reach it. This paper builds a reproducible classification of 23 cryptoassets across ten economic functions: monetary use, gas demand, staking, burn, scarcity, collateral, governance, protocol capture, utility and incentives. All 230 decisions use written rules, dated evidence and explicit exclusions; architecture is context rather than a function. Across 253 asset pairs, same-architecture pairs share 51.7 percent of functions on average versus 33.1 percent across architectures, showing that architecture is informative but incomplete. The classification is useful today as a due-diligence instrument, not as a standalone pricing model. In the verified cross-section, financial integration lowers leave-one-asset-out valuation error by 2.8 percent relative to simply counting functions, a modest exploratory improvement. Network activity has a positive one-year association with value but is asset-sensitive. Fee activity appears more informative when a capture route is present, yet the result disappears under the stricter rule requiring value to terminate at the token or holder. Seventeen material unlock and burn events across three assets do not support a standalone supply-event trading rule. The staking-liquidity test remains unestimated because historical Ethereum active stake is unavailable. The defensible contribution is a transparent way to identify what an asset does, distinguish usage from holder capture, compare economically similar assets and reject unsupported narratives. All results and classifications are regenerated by one tested pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cryptoassets; cryptocurrency valuation; token economics; value capture; economic classification; reproducible research

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The problem}
Valuing an equity begins with agreement about what kind of claim it is. Cryptoassets have no equivalent agreement. They are grouped by the technology beneath them, so that Bitcoin and Ethereum are both ``Layer 1'' despite opposite monetary designs: Bitcoin has a fixed terminal supply and no fee destruction, Ethereum has no supply cap and destroys fees as they are paid. They are grouped by application category, so that Uniswap and Aave are both ``DeFi tokens'' while sharing one economic function out of ten. Neither grouping states how the asset is supposed to accrue value, and neither can be tested.

Formal theory has advanced faster than measurement. \citet{congliwang2021tokenomics} model token value as arising from adoption in a platform economy, and the empirical crypto asset-pricing literature has established a small set of price-based factors \citep{liutsyvinski2021risks,liutsyvinskiwu2022common}. What is missing between them is an instrument: a way to say what economic job a given token performs on a given date, defensibly enough that the classification can be an independent variable.

\subsection{What this paper builds}
We build that instrument and then test it. The research question is:

\begin{quote}
Do the economic functions a token actually performs explain what it is worth and how risky it is, beyond what its architecture and its size already imply?
\end{quote}

The approach has three commitments that distinguish it from existing descriptive taxonomies.

\textbf{Rules before cases.} Each of ten functions is defined by a written classification rule and a list of the evidence that satisfies it. A code is set to one only when the mechanism is live at the observation date, economically material, and supported by dated primary evidence. Unknown states remain null and never become zero.

\textbf{Dates on everything.} Token economics change. Uniswap did not route fees to a burn until December 2025; Aave began a buyback in April 2025 and paused it in April 2026. Applying today's classification to yesterday's prices imports future information into past outcomes. Every mechanism therefore carries an effective date and the panel resolves the state prevailing on each observation day.

\textbf{Specifications before estimates.} Each experiment has a specification file committed with a freeze date before its estimate is produced, naming outcomes, predictors, samples, inference procedure and multiple-testing treatment. Two results in this paper became weaker when those rules were applied honestly, which is the evidence that the rules bind.

\subsection{Contributions}
First, a completed classification instrument with an auditable evidence trail: 230 of 230 decisions across 23 active assets are evidence-backed. Independent blind review covers ten decisions and cannot be generalized to the full matrix. Second, a practical distinction between network usage and holder capture, including the governed-treasury boundary that materially changes the H2 estimate. Third, direct empirical checks of popular valuation narratives, including negative evidence against standalone unlock and burn signals. Fourth, a decision-oriented advisory output that states what was measured, what was learned and what action is defensible. Fifth, an execution pipeline of 97 modules in seven ordered stages, with immutable raw snapshots and 388 unit tests, so every number below is regenerable from one command \citep{repo2026}.

\section{Key Findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Architecture helps, but cannot replace economic classification.} Same-architecture pairs share 51.7 percent of their economic functions on average, against 33.1 percent for cross-architecture pairs (100,000-draw label-permutation $p=0.00023$). Nevertheless, HBAR--TAO have identical function profiles across different architectures and BNB--TRX overlap by 88.9 percent.

\item \textbf{The central capture result is definition-dependent.} Fee activity is more strongly associated with market value where a live capture route exists: pooled interaction $0.132$, exact eleven-cluster $p = 0.082$, positive in all eleven leave-one-asset-out samples. Applying the strict holder-capture rule to the same panel halves the estimate to $0.061$ with $p = 0.478$. Whether a tokenholder-governed treasury counts as capture is the difference between a result and no result.

\item \textbf{Which functions are present is more useful than how many.} Across the completed universe, financial integration has leave-one-asset-out RMSE $1.759$ against $1.811$ for raw function breadth, a 2.8 percent reduction. Monetary/store use, supply absorption and financial integration survive the exploratory ten-percent false-discovery screen. This is a cross-sectional research lead, not a price target.

\item \textbf{Supply events are not standalone trading signals.} Across seventeen material unlock and burn events for ARB, BNB and UNI, eight carry the predicted sign and all three asset-level mean direction-adjusted returns oppose the hypothesis. The exact asset-level sign-flip $p$-value is $0.25$.

\item \textbf{A single market factor dominates.} Median market beta $1.03$ and median $R^2$ of $0.60$ across twenty-four assets. Daily Fama--MacBeth cross-sections give a negative lagged-beta premium ($t = -2.12$), flat momentum and no volatility premium, echoing the betting-against-beta pattern of \citet{frazzinipedersen2014}.

\item \textbf{Function and risk move together but do not survive correction.} Assets classified as monetary average beta $0.93$ and worst log drawdown $-1.44$ against $1.19$ and $-2.37$ for the rest, with the same ordering for transaction-service demand and the reverse for governance. Three of twenty-seven tests reach $p \le 0.05$ against $1.35$ expected by chance and none survives a ten percent false-discovery rate.

\item \textbf{The usable output today is the classification, not a trading algorithm.} Advisors can use it to identify economic jobs, compare like with like, separate usage from capture and expose missing evidence. The current tests do not support price targets, portfolio weights or automatic buy/sell signals.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Classification and effective dating}
Let $c_{i,k}(t) \in \{0,1,\varnothing\}$ denote the state of function $k$ for asset $i$ on date $t$. Time-varying codes are resolved from an event ledger $\mathcal{E}$ of tuples $(i,k,\tau,v)$ where $\tau$ is an effective date:
\begin{equation}
c_{i,k}(t) = v\big(\arg\max\{\tau : (i,k,\tau,v) \in \mathcal{E},\ \tau \le t\}\big),
\end{equation}
so only evidence in force on or before $t$ can determine the state at $t$. A pause is an event with $v=0$; an announced-but-inactive mechanism generates no event. Eight bundles $\mathcal{B}_b$ partition the ten codes, fixed before estimation, giving bundle indicators and raw breadth
\begin{equation}
B_{i,b}(t) = \max_{k \in \mathcal{B}_b} c_{i,k}(t), \qquad
N_i(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{10} c_{i,k}(t).
\end{equation}
Active capture, the H2 treatment, is $C_{it} = \max\{c_{i,\mathrm{BURN}}(t),\, c_{i,\mathrm{PROTOCOL}}(t)\}$.

\subsection{Classification reliability}
A second reviewer scores the same cells blind. With observed agreement $p_o$ and chance agreement $p_e$ computed from the marginal distributions, Cohen's $\kappa$ \citep{cohen1960kappa} is
\begin{equation}
\kappa = \frac{p_o - p_e}{1 - p_e},
\end{equation}
interpreted on the \citet{landiskoch1977} scale. Disagreements are adjudicated by a written rule that is then binding on all future cases.

\subsection{H2: usage interacted with value capture}
The panel specification is a two-way fixed-effects regression
\begin{equation}
y_{it} = \alpha_i + \gamma_t + \beta_1 F_{i,t-1} + \beta_2 C_{it} + \beta_3\big(F_{i,t-1} \times C_{it}\big) + \beta_4\big(F_{i,t-1} \times A_i\big) + \varepsilon_{it},
\label{eq:h2}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is log lagged fees, $A_i$ marks application-level rather than chain-level fee measurement, and $y$ is either log market capitalization or the seven-day forward log return. The parameter of interest is $\beta_3$. Estimation uses the within transformation $\tilde{x}_{it} = x_{it} - \bar{x}_{i\cdot} - \bar{x}_{\cdot t} + \bar{x}$, which absorbs $\alpha_i$ and $\gamma_t$; the time-invariant scope main effect is absorbed by the asset effect.

With $G$ asset clusters the CR1 cluster-robust variance is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{V} = \frac{G}{G-1}\cdot\frac{N-1}{N-K}\,(X'X)^{-1}\Big(\sum_{g=1}^{G} X_g'\hat{u}_g\hat{u}_g'X_g\Big)(X'X)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Because $G$ is small, asymptotic cluster inference is unreliable \citep{cameron2008bootstrap,mackinnonwebb2017}. We therefore impose $H_0:\beta_3=0$, obtain restricted fitted values and residuals $(\hat{y}^r,\hat{u}^r)$, and for every one of the $2^G$ Rademacher sign vectors $v \in \{-1,+1\}^G$ construct
\begin{equation}
y^*_{it} = \hat{y}^r_{it} + v_{g(i)}\hat{u}^r_{it},
\end{equation}
re-estimate, and form the bootstrap $t$ statistic. The two-sided $p$-value is the exact enumeration
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{1}{2^G}\sum_{b=1}^{2^G}\mathbf{1}\{|t^*_b| \ge |t_{\mathrm{obs}}|\},
\end{equation}
with $2^{11}=2048$ assignments pooled and $2^{9}=512$ for the nine-chain sample, so attainable $p$-values are coarse but exact rather than approximate \citep{roodman2019boottest}. Seven-day forward returns overlap; we therefore partition dates into all seven calendar offsets, each internally spaced seven days apart, and report the offsets separately rather than choosing one.

The strict-rule sensitivity re-estimates \eqref{eq:h2} on a copy of the panel in which pre-listed classification overrides are applied. The frozen panel, its evidence files and its ledger are not modified.

\subsection{Cross-sectional tests and permutation inference}
Breadth, bundle and risk tests are cross-sectional with $n$ assets, so we use exact randomization inference \citep{fisher1935design}. For a statistic $\theta$ computed from predictor $x$ and outcome $y$,
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi \in S_n}\mathbf{1}\{|\theta(x_{\pi},y)| \ge |\theta(x,y)|\},
\end{equation}
enumerated exactly when $n \le 6$ ($6! = 720$) and estimated by $R = 20{,}000$ fixed-seed Monte Carlo draws when $n = 20$. Sign stability is reported by re-estimating on every leave-one-asset-out subsample. Model comparison uses leave-one-out root mean squared prediction error,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{LOO}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{n}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\hat{y}^{(-i)}_i - y_i\big)^2},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{y}^{(-i)}_i$ is the prediction for asset $i$ from a model fitted without it, so a richer model is not rewarded for fitting its own sample.

\subsection{P1\_H6: function against realized risk}
All assets are measured on one common window so that risk measures are comparable. For asset $i$ over that window, with $r_{it}$ the daily log return and $r_{mt}$ the equal-weighted market log return,
\begin{align}
\beta_i &= \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(r_{it},r_{mt})}{\mathrm{Var}(r_{mt})},
&\sigma_i &= \mathrm{sd}(r_{it})\sqrt{365},
&\mathrm{MDD}_i &= \min_{T}\Big(\sum_{t \le T} r_{it} - \max_{U \le T}\sum_{t \le U} r_{it}\Big).
\end{align}
Each outcome is regressed on one classification predictor and a size control $v_i$, the mean log dollar volume, because the largest assets are also the lowest-beta assets:
\begin{equation}
y_i = a + b\,x_i + c\,v_i + e_i .
\end{equation}
Predictors with no cross-asset variation are reported as unestimable rather than dropped. Across the $m = 27$ predictor-by-outcome tests within each sample we control the false discovery rate \citep{benjaminihochberg1995,harvey2016cross}: with ordered $p$-values $p_{(1)} \le \cdots \le p_{(m)}$,
\begin{equation}
q_{(j)} = \min_{\ell \ge j}\ \min\Big\{1,\ \frac{m\,p_{(\ell)}}{\ell}\Big\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Factor baseline}
Before any classification variable is tested we establish what price-based factors already explain. Per-asset market models give $\beta_i$ and $R^2_i$. Daily cross-sections follow \citet{famamacbeth1973}: for each date $t$,
\begin{equation}
r_{it} = \lambda_{0t} + \sum_{k}\lambda_{kt}\,x_{i,k,t-1} + e_{it},
\end{equation}
with predictors lagged one day; the reported premium is $\bar{\lambda}_k = T^{-1}\sum_t \lambda_{kt}$ and its standard error is autocorrelation-consistent \citep{neweywest1987},
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar{\lambda}_k) = \frac{1}{T}\Big[\hat{\gamma}_0 + 2\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\Big(1-\frac{\ell}{L+1}\Big)\hat{\gamma}_\ell\Big],
\qquad \hat{\gamma}_\ell = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t>\ell}(\lambda_{kt}-\bar{\lambda}_k)(\lambda_{k,t-\ell}-\bar{\lambda}_k),
\end{equation}
with $L = 7$.

\subsection{Mechanism event study}
Events are every ledger transition whose state differs from the preceding recorded state and whose full pre and post window lies inside the panel; an initial coding at an asset's first evidence date is not an event. For treated asset $i$, control set $\mathcal{C}$ of assets with no ledger event inside the window, and windows of $W$ days either side of $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta_j = \frac{1}{W}\sum_{t=\tau+1}^{\tau+W} y_{jt} - \frac{1}{W}\sum_{t=\tau-W}^{\tau-1} y_{jt},
\qquad
\mathrm{DiD} = \Delta_i - \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{C}}\Delta_j .
\end{equation}
Two placebo distributions are constructed. The asset placebo reassigns treatment to each control in turn; the calendar placebo shifts $\tau$ by multiples of seven days to windows that do not overlap the true event window. In each case
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{\#\{|\mathrm{DiD}_{\text{placebo}}| \ge |\mathrm{DiD}_{\text{obs}}|\} + 1}{\#\text{placebos} + 1},
\end{equation}
counting the observed statistic in its own reference distribution. The same statistic is computed for lagged fees, so that a valuation move is not attributed to capture when usage moved simultaneously.

\subsection{Architecture versus economic similarity}
For every one of the ${23 \choose 2}=253$ asset pairs, we compute Jaccard similarity across the ten binary function codes. The 30 same-architecture pairs average $0.517$, compared with $0.331$ for the 223 cross-architecture pairs, a difference of $0.186$. Permuting the observed architecture labels 100,000 times gives a two-sided $p$-value of $0.00023$. Architecture therefore contains real information about economic design. It is nevertheless incomplete: HBAR--TAO have identical ten-function profiles despite different architecture labels, while BNB--TRX overlap by $0.889$, BCH--XLM by $0.800$, and ETH--SOL by $0.750$. For advisory comparison, architecture should be retained as an operational-risk descriptor, while economic-function overlap selects the primary peers.

\subsection{The governed-treasury capture boundary}
ARB and ADA tokenholders exercise binding control over treasuries funded by network revenue, but the evidenced disbursements fund ecosystem grants, infrastructure and development \citep{cardano2026cip1694,arbitrum2026transparency}. Under the strict rule both are governance, not capture, because value never mechanically reaches the token. Fee destruction is capture, because it does: the XRP Ledger states that the transaction cost ``is not paid to any party'' and the XRP ``is irrevocably destroyed'' \citep{xrpl2026cost}, and Solana burns half of every base fee \citep{solana2026fees}. The same boundary settles Zcash, whose development fund routes newly issued subsidy to organizations rather than to the token \citep{zcash2026zip1014}.

\paragraph{H1, H3, H4.} The H1 design was frozen before expanding the activity sample. Its primary nine-native-network panel contains 792 observations over 88 common dates. Active addresses have a positive coefficient of $0.0378$ (exact nine-cluster $p=0.344$), while transaction count has a negative coefficient of $-0.0420$ ($p=0.137$). A subsequently frozen retrospective one-year extension keeps the same assets and model and covers 3,258 observations over 362 dates. Both coefficients become positive: $0.1748$ for addresses ($p=0.184$) and $0.3208$ for transactions ($p=0.078$). The latter is suggestive but turns slightly negative when XLM is omitted, and the backward extension is not independent validation. A twelve-asset sensitivity that mixes native-network and ERC-20 token-contract activity is not used as primary evidence. The supply diagnostic (296 observations) has a positive rather than the hypothesized negative coefficient, $p = 0.250$. A separately frozen event study estimates twelve material ARB unlocks, four official BNB quarterly burns, and the completed 100 million UNI burn. All three asset-level mean direction-adjusted returns oppose H3; eight of seventeen events match their predicted sign and the exact asset-level sign-flip $p$-value is $0.25$. The asset-level randomization avoids treating repeated events as independent, but three assets provide only eight sign assignments. The UNI execution was preceded by proposal and vote stages, so its execution-date effect may be attenuated by anticipation. An evidence-only fourth-asset screen, conducted without consulting returns, rejected OP because its documented event was below the frozen 0.5\% materiality threshold, SUI because an exact dated amount was unavailable, and AVAX because its burn is continuous rather than a discrete event. Before H4 estimation, an impossible three-positive-series gate was corrected: the fixed universe contains only two comparable consensus-staking systems. The frozen primary panel is therefore ETH and BNB plus BTC, UNI and ARB structural controls; legacy stkAAVE remains a separate case study. All market inputs and BNB stake cover the 362-day common window, but the exact Ethereum effective-balance collector still requires an archival consensus endpoint, so estimation remains blocked.

\subsection{Classification coverage and what remains}
The active universe contains 23 assets and all $23 \times 10 = 230$ cells are evidence-backed: 220 sourced decisions and ten sourced decisions with an independent blind review. DOGE and XMR are preserved in the audit trail but excluded from estimation. Coverage is complete, but independent reproducibility is not: the blind review covers only burn and protocol-capture decisions for ADA, AVAX, SOL, TRX, and XRP, so its reliability statistic cannot be generalized to the matrix.

\subsection{Exploratory relative valuation models (RV1)}
The next modelling layer asks whether economic functions and measured fundamentals predict relative asset size. Its specification was committed before estimation, but historical outcomes had already been examined; this is an exploratory extension rather than independent confirmation of H8. Current classifications are retrospective asset descriptors and do not reconstruct historical information sets. The outcome is mean log market capitalization over 25 August 2025--22 August 2026. All-group and raw-count models use the same 22 assets; fundamentals models use the same 11 assets with sufficient fees and volume history.

We estimate ridge regression with an unpenalized intercept and fixed penalty of 10. Predictors are standardized using training assets alone. Every asset is held out of its own fit. We compare held-out root mean squared error (RMSE) and mean absolute error; no model is selected for deployment. For each held-out asset, inner leave-one-asset-out absolute errors from its training set supply a 90th-percentile diagnostic log-error band. These empirical bands have no calibrated prediction coverage guarantee. Predictor contributions reconstruct each fitted log estimate and are descriptive rather than causal.

\subsection{Traditional-asset comparison and advisory synthesis (CA1)}
The separately frozen CA1 extension compares a fixed equal-weight basket of 21 eligible cryptoassets with adjusted returns on GLD, SPY and IWM as proxies for gold, the S\&P 500 and Russell 2000. HYPE has no source history and TON fails the predeclared 90 percent calendar coverage rule. Membership is retrospective. The analysis uses 52 weekly return intervals between matched session endpoints from 22 August 2025 through 21 August 2026, compounding every intervening crypto calendar return. Holidays are not forward-filled. Crypto UTC closes and ETF New York closes remain asynchronous; plus/minus-one-day shifts quantify timing sensitivity.

Weekly Pearson correlations for the crypto basket are 0.110 with GLD, 0.361 with SPY and 0.282 with IWM. Marginal 95 percent percentile intervals from 2,000 paired circular block-bootstrap draws in four-week blocks are respectively $[-0.189,0.411]$, $[0.077,0.653]$ and $[-0.018,0.580]$. These are exploratory uncertainty intervals, not simultaneous significance claims. Rank correlations, matched daily returns, chronological halves, rolling 60-session windows, individual assets and overlapping economic groups are reported as descriptive sensitivities. Gold changes correlation sign across sample halves, and small-cap co-movement is timing-sensitive.

During the 21 SPY-negative weeks, the crypto basket lost in two-thirds of observations and averaged a negative 3.3 percent weekly return. The worst-quintile regime contains only 11 weeks, below the threshold for reporting its correlation. Weak average correlation therefore provides no evidence of reliable downside protection. The one-year sample, USDT/USD basis differences, ETF-versus-index tracking, retrospective membership and single-vendor benchmark prices constrain generalization. A generated advisory report combines these results with the classification matrix, existing hypotheses, RV1 estimates, technical context and four companion figures. The report is independent research for advisory discussion; external circulation requires review of data provenance and interpretation.

\section{Limitations}
All active-universe classifications are evidence-backed, but only ten cells have an independent blind review, so reproducibility by another coder remains untested. Nineteen assets meet the common risk-window rule, but the verified refresh is not independent confirmation because the outcomes were observed before the classification-only rerun. Eleven H2 asset clusters remain few even with exact enumeration, and only two application protocols are represented. Four of ten functions are documentary rather than measured and cannot yet enter a panel. The common risk window begins at a 2023 listing date and therefore excludes the 2022 drawdown. Fee, revenue and holder-revenue concepts retain provider definitions \citep{defillama2026definitions} and are not interchangeable. Free-tier activity data covers a subset of assets. Classifications are research codes, not legal conclusions, and every result is an association rather than a causal or investment claim.

\section{Conclusion}
The concrete output today is a reproducible economic due-diligence framework. It tells an advisor what jobs a token performs, whether usage can reach holders, which supply mechanisms are live, and which assets are economically comparable. This is more defensible than relying on labels such as Layer 1, DeFi or utility token.

The empirical evidence narrows how the framework should be used. Function composition modestly outpredicts raw function count, with financial integration the strongest exploratory indicator. Usage alone is unstable, and apparent value-capture results depend on whether value actually terminates at the token or holder. Unlocks and burns do not work as standalone trading signals in the present event sample. Function-based risk differences do not survive multiple-testing correction, and staking cannot yet be tested without Ethereum history. The appropriate action is therefore to use the matrix for structured comparison and narrative discipline while withholding price targets, portfolio weights and trading rules until the fixed models succeed in later data.

\section{Classification Rules}
A code is set to one only when the mechanism is live at the observation date, economically material, supported by dated primary evidence, and linked to token demand, supply or holder rights.
\begin{description}
  \item[VA\_MONETARY] Material money, settlement, reserve or store-of-value use supported by at least two behavioral tests.
  \item[VA\_GAS] The native asset is protocol-required to settle ordinary execution, storage, transaction or blockspace fees; relay policy and optional discounts do not qualify.
  \item[VA\_STAKE] Active bonding or delegation secures consensus, a material service or protocol risk absorption, exposing capital to protocol-defined consequences.
  \item[VA\_BURN] A live rules-based mechanism irreversibly destroys tokens.
  \item[VA\_SCARCITY] A credible hard cap or deterministic terminal bound exists and is exceptionally difficult to change; burn alone and discretionary targets do not qualify.
  \item[VA\_COLLATERAL] Collateral balances of at least one percent of market capitalization or one hundred million dollars for at least ninety days.
  \item[VA\_GOV] Holding or delegating provides live proposal, voting, veto, treasury or parameter control.
  \item[VA\_PROTOCOL] A live mechanical route sends protocol revenue or surplus to the token or holders through distribution, buyback, burn or an enforceable holder claim. Governance over ecosystem spending alone does not qualify.
  \item[VA\_UTILITY] The token is required to consume an identifiable non-generic service beyond transfer.
  \item[VA\_INCENTIVE] A systematic rules-based programme subsidizes liquidity, usage, development or participation; consensus issuance and discretionary grants do not qualify.
\end{description}

\section{Bundle Map}
Monetary store (MONETARY, SCARCITY); transaction service demand (GAS, UTILITY); security commitment (STAKE); supply absorption (BURN); financial integration (COLLATERAL); control rights (GOV); holder capture (PROTOCOL); subsidized participation (INCENTIVE). Every function belongs to exactly one bundle and the map was fixed before estimation.
