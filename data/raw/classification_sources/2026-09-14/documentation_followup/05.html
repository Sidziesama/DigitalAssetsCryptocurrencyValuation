What is Gleyo?
Gleyo is a Zcash-native community growth platform designed to help communities onboard and retain contributors through quests, engagement workflows, and ZEC rewards.
Instead of requiring newcomers to understand wallets and shielded transactions before participating, Gleyo allows communities to reward contributors in shielded ZEC as they participate.

What has shipped

  • Gleyo launched on Zcash mainnet on July 14, 2026
  • 38+ weekly active users in closed beta
  • Real mainnet ZEC deposits, quest rewards, and shielded withdrawals
  • Multi-token funding through USDT/USDC → ZEC conversion
  • Production withdrawal architecture designed to safely handle concurrent community activity
  • Full end-to-end compatibility with the Ironwood network upgrade
  • Contributed an upstream fix to Nozy Wallet (PR #180) that restored Ironwood pool deposit compatibility
  • Continued development and ecosystem testing following the ZecHub Hackathon

Ecosystem impact
Gleyo has been tested by members of the Zcash ecosystem, including Zcash Nigeria, ZecForge, and PGPZ, with ongoing discussions around using Gleyo for community and ambassador programs.
The project also contributes directly to the Gleyo GitHub: https://github.com/gilmorre/gleyo-Zechub-oader Zcash ecosystem through its integration with Nozy Wallet and upstream technical contributions.

Gleyo - Additional Progress Since Application

Since submitting the retroactive grant application, I’ve continued building and testing Gleyo and wanted to share a few additional signals from the past couple of weeks.

User growth and activity

Gleyo has now reached 43 registered users. More importantly, I’m starting to see more users actually going through the platform, completing quests, interacting with communities, and testing different flows.

Some of the newer users have also been giving direct feedback about wanting more quests and more things to do on the platform. That has pushed me to start creating and updating quests more regularly so there is a reason for users to keep coming back.

This has been particularly useful for validating one of Gleyo’s original goals: moving beyond simply acquiring users toward actually retaining and engaging them.

Growing interest from communities

I’m also seeing more interest from communities that want to explore using Gleyo for their own onboarding, quests, and engagement workflows.

This gives me an opportunity to test Gleyo across different community needs rather than building around a single use case.

There’s also something new coming soon around the onboarding and community growth side that I’ve been working heavily on recently.

Continued ecosystem support

I’ve continued receiving valuable feedback, testing, and encouragement from builders in the Zcash ecosystem, particularly within ZecHub. I’ve been able to work closely with @squirrel, @dismad, and @lowo88, whose feedback and discussions have helped me identify areas to improve and think more deeply about Gleyo’s direction.

What I’m learning

One of the biggest lessons from having real users is that shipping a feature is only the beginning. Seeing where users get confused, what they want to do next, and what brings them back has been giving me much stronger signals about what Gleyo needs to become.

I’ll continue sharing progress and evidence as development continues.

43 users today, more activity, more feedback, more quests, and more still to build.

Gleyo Update: Early Community Traction and What We’re Learning From Onboarding

Since my retroactive grant application, Gleyo has continued to see meaningful traction as communities and new users begin using the platform.

Gleyo has now reached 155 users, and we are beginning to see activity move beyond testing into real community use.

One recent example is the Proof of Privacy community, which is now using Gleyo to onboard participants and run interactive quests around its Zcash education activities.

The platform has also continued generating conversations with other communities around how they could use Gleyo for onboarding, engagement, and participation. One piece of feedback from the Zcash Ghana Residency Program organiser was the idea of supporting coinholder voting directly on Gleyo. I implemented the functionality, and we plan to explore its use as the platform continues developing.

What we’re learning about onboarding

One of the most interesting things for me has been seeing how new users respond to educational quests.

Gleyo recently asked users to explain Zcash in simple terms, as if they were telling a friend. Some of the responses showed that participants were able to understand and communicate the core idea behind Zcash, particularly the combination of decentralized money and privacy, even when they had not yet developed a deep understanding of technical concepts such as shielded transactions.

For example, one participant described it as:

“Bitcoin focuses on being decentralized money; Zcash adds strong privacy to that idea.”

That is an important learning signal for Gleyo’s onboarding approach.

It suggests that newcomers do not necessarily need to understand every technical prerequisite before they can begin engaging with the Zcash ecosystem. Interactive quests can help users first understand the core value proposition, then progressively introduce deeper concepts as they continue participating.

This is the direction Gleyo was designed around: reducing the initial complexity of entering a new ecosystem while creating a path for users to learn more over time.

Current activity

Recent platform activity has included:

  • 154 total users
  • Active community onboarding through quests
  • 302 quests started
  • 281 quests completed
  • A 93% completion rate across the current 7-day analytics view
  • Growing community chat activity and participation

The most encouraging part is that these numbers are now being accompanied by real qualitative feedback and community use cases. Gleyo is increasingly helping me understand not only whether people complete onboarding flows, but also what they actually understand after completing them.

I will continue sharing updates as more communities onboard to Gleyo and use it to run real onboarding and engagement activities, while we gather more data around onboarding, engagement, and long-term retention.

Gleyo Update - From Building to Seeing It Used

It’s been a while since I shared an update here.

The past couple of weeks have been pretty intense. A lot of my time has gone into working directly with communities, supporting programmes running on Gleyo, fixing things as they come up, and figuring out what Gleyo needs to become more useful beyond my own initial ideas for the product.

And honestly, the biggest change since my last update has been seeing Gleyo actually start to become part of how communities operate.

We’re now seeing communities use Gleyo for things like onboarding, education, developer programmes, quests, and community engagement.

That shift has been really encouraging.

Gleyo started as something I built during the ZecHub Hackathon because I thought there was a better way to help communities onboard and retain people.

Now I’m getting to see that idea tested in the real world.

Proof of Privacy

The recent Proof of Privacy campaign was another major milestone for Gleyo.

The campaign brought a significant number of people through the platform and generated 125 Zodl downloads associated with the campaign experience.

There were also a lot of useful things I learned from watching how participants actually used Gleyo throughout the campaign.

I’ll leave the full campaign results to the Proof of Privacy team when they publish their own update, but I’m really happy with what the campaign has shown from the Gleyo side.

Developer onboarding

We’re also seeing Gleyo being used in a completely different context through the Zcash Privacy Developers Residency in Ghana.

Participants are now using Gleyo for practical developer activities, moving from learning into actually completing tasks and submitting work.

This is particularly interesting to me because it shows that the same infrastructure can support different stages of someone’s journey into Zcash.

Someone can discover Zcash → learn about it → participate in a community → complete practical tasks → and eventually become a contributor.

That’s increasingly the direction I’m thinking about for Gleyo.

What’s changing for me

When I first started building Gleyo, most of the questions were:

“Can I build this?”

Now the questions are becoming:

“Can communities actually use this effectively?”

“Can someone who knows nothing about Zcash understand where to start?”

“Can we turn a new user into someone who keeps participating?”

“Can communities run their own programmes without needing me involved in every step?”

Those are much more interesting problems to solve.

And the answers are becoming clearer with every community that uses Gleyo.

There is still a lot to improve, especially around reliability, security, infrastructure, and making the platform easier for communities to operate independently.

But we’re getting much closer to the product I originally imagined.

Gleyo is no longer just something I’m building. It’s something people are starting to use.

Still early.

Still building.

More soon.
